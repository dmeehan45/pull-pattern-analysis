# Evidence model

## Evidence is not binary

Use descriptive evidence states rather than a universal numerical PULL score.

### Stated interest
The actor expresses enthusiasm, preference, or hypothetical use.

Useful context; weak commitment evidence.

### Problem evidence
A concrete Project, constraint, or failure in current options is described.

Stronger than abstract interest but may still be passive.

### Resource expenditure
The actor is spending money, time, headcount, political capital, or operational effort.

Meaningful PULL evidence.

### Active search/evaluation
The actor is comparing alternatives, contacting vendors, testing workarounds, recruiting help, or otherwise investing in finding an option.

### Buying-shaped action
The actor initiates behavior needed for a transaction or implementation.

Examples:
- brings in approver;
- starts security review;
- provides data;
- allocates pilot staff;
- starts procurement;
- negotiates terms.

### Transaction
Resources are formally allocated or change hands.

Strong evidence for that actor and context. Still not proof of repeatability.

## Provenance fields

Every material evidence item should retain:
- source ID;
- source date;
- actor ID;
- episode ID;
- organization/context;
- collection channel;
- original excerpt or faithful source reference;
- evidence state;
- observed/reported/inferred classification;
- analyst notes.

## Denominators matter

The agent must distinguish:

> "7 anecdotes support this pattern"

from:

> "7 of 8 independent cases with this forcing condition support it"

and from:

> "7 selected closed-won customers support it; non-buyers were not included."

These are not equivalent.

When the denominator is unknown, say so.

## Contradictions are first-class data

Never smooth contradictions away during synthesis.

Store:
- the conflicting claims;
- which sources support each;
- whether the conflict can be explained by time, role, organization, or context;
- what evidence would resolve it.

## Temporal provenance

Demand evidence has a half-life.

Preserve the date because:
- budgets reset;
- regulations change;
- incumbents improve;
- roles change;
- market shocks pass;
- internal priorities move.

A previously strong pattern may become stale without becoming historically false.

## Source quality does not equal source positivity

A detailed negative case may be more informative than an enthusiastic positive case.

The agent should prioritize evidence by its ability to discriminate among explanations, not by whether it supports the emerging story.
